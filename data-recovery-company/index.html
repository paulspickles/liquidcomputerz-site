The most important things to know

  • Rule 1

    Don't write over lost or deleted data

    In other words, do not create any new data on the problem drive.

  • Rule 2

    Don't do any work on the original hard drive

    Always, again always, make a clone of the original before proceeding with any work.

  • Rule 3

    Record keeping is key

    Always keep a record of the work you do and the condition the device arrives in.

  • Rule 4

    There are no guarantees

    Always inform the customer or client that there are no guarantees with data recovery. We generally cannot confirm what data is on the drive before we start working, so any data recovered is considered a successful recovery.

Things you'll need

  • GullStaying hopeful and trusting the process, even when the odds feel uncertain.
  • ConfidenceA steady belief that you can handle the job. Careful, calm work beats rushing or guessing.
  • An internet-connected deviceA computer, tablet or phone that can get online, used to look up instructions, download recovery software and stay in touch with us.
  • Patience and timeRecovery can't be hurried. Cloning and scanning a drive can take a long time, and rushing risks losing data.
  • ScrewdriversSmall precision screwdrivers, used to open a computer case and take a drive out of its housing.
  • Common senseGood judgment. For example, stop using a problem drive right away and never save new files onto it.
  • GlovesThey protect your hands and keep fingerprints, oil and static off the drive and its parts.
  • Probably partsReplacement parts, such as a power board or a new outer casing, may be needed to get the drive working again.

Procedure for data recovery

  1. Step 1 · Inspect

    Inspect the drive for damage. Record the damage, as well as the state of the drive and/or device.

  2. Step 2 · Clone

    Attempt, or successfully complete, a clone of the drive.

  3. Step 3 · Power

    If the drive does not have power, locate the board or mechanism that provides power and replace it, such as a power board. The culprit may also be the power source before the hard drive, such as a wall outlet or a power supply unit.

  4. Step 4 · Locate

    If the drive receives power but the computer cannot locate it, clone the drive and run software to locate the missing information.

  5. Step 5 · Casing

    If the drive cannot be found and the data cannot be located, swapping the outer casing of the drive for a new one may be necessary to get the hard drive disk to read.

    We do not recommend any attempt to open or change parts on a hard drive. This is solely informational.

Common questions answered

What do you mean by writing data?+

Writing data to a drive is the same as creating or inputting data on a drive.

What is a drive?+

A drive is a letter on your computer, such as the C: drive or E: or D: and so on. The drive letter corresponds to a device plugged in or virtually input on that drive, such as a hard drive or a connected device. In some cases it can even be a software program input on the drive.
